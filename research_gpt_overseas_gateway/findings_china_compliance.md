# Mainland China compliance findings: overseas LLM gateway

Research date: 2026-08-02 (Asia/Shanghai). This is a product/compliance research summary, not legal advice.

## Bottom line

A vendor-controlled gateway can technically relay GPT requests so mainland users do not install a VPN. For a China-facing commercial product, however, the design is not merely a connectivity feature: prompts, attachments, account metadata, and logs may be personal information or important data sent abroad, and the product may itself be a mainland-facing generative-AI service. Hosting the relay overseas does not remove those obligations. A production launch should therefore be gated on China-qualified counsel, a documented data map and personal-information protection impact assessment, the applicable cross-border transfer route, and the generative-AI service assessment/filing analysis.

## 1. Personal information and cross-border transfer

- The Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL) applies to processing in China and can also apply to overseas processing aimed at offering products/services to people in China (Arts. 3-4). A prompt or file containing information about an identified or identifiable person is personal information; transmission, storage and disclosure are all processing. Encryption or pseudonymization reduces risk but is not the same as legal anonymization.
- Baseline duties still apply before considering data-export thresholds: lawful basis, a clear and reasonable purpose, minimum necessary collection, transparency, shortest necessary retention, security controls, and user rights (Arts. 5-9, 13-19, 44-51). If an overseas LLM/provider is an entrusted processor, the operator must contractually define purpose, duration, method, data types, safeguards, rights/obligations and supervise it (Art. 21). If the provider independently uses prompts (for example, for training), the role and consent analysis may be more demanding.
- A transfer abroad ordinarily requires specific notice of the overseas recipient's identity/contact details, purpose, method, data categories, and the method for exercising rights, plus the individual's **separate consent** (Art. 39). The operator must also conduct and record a personal-information protection impact assessment before exporting personal information; the report/record must be retained for at least three years (Arts. 55-56). Generic wording such as “may share with third-party AI providers” is unlikely to satisfy the specificity the statute calls for.
- A user may paste another person's personal or sensitive information into a prompt or attachment. The uploader cannot always provide a valid consent on behalf of that other person. Product controls should therefore include prominent warnings, configurable redaction/DLP, blocked or separately confirmed sensitive-data classes, tenant policies, minimal logs, short retention, and deletion/access workflows—not just a privacy-policy checkbox.
- PIPL Art. 38 establishes the main transfer mechanisms: CAC security assessment, certification, or the CAC standard contract (plus other legally specified routes), and requires protection at the overseas recipient to reach the PIPL standard. The 2024 CAC regulation adjusts when the formal mechanisms are required. For a non-CIIO operator, calculated from January 1 of the current year:
  - under 100,000 people’s non-sensitive personal information: exempt from security assessment / standard contract / certification, provided no important data or sensitive personal information is involved;
  - 100,000 to under 1,000,000 people’s non-sensitive personal information, or under 10,000 people’s sensitive personal information: standard contract or certification;
  - 1,000,000 or more people’s non-sensitive personal information, or 10,000 or more people’s sensitive personal information: CAC security assessment;
  - a CIIO exporting personal information/important data, or any non-CIIO exporting important data: CAC security assessment.
- These threshold exemptions do **not** waive substantive privacy duties. The same 2024 regulation expressly preserves notice, separate consent, impact assessment, and data-security measures (Arts. 10-11). It also says data not identified to the operator by the relevant authority/region, or publicly announced, as important data need not be submitted as important data (Art. 2); classification should still be documented rather than assumed.
- Since 2026-01-01, the Personal Information Export Certification Measures govern the certification route. They reiterate prior notice, separate consent, impact assessment and assessment of the overseas recipient's legal environment and safeguards. Certification is one route, not a blanket approval for an overseas LLM.

## 2. Generative-AI service rules

- The Interim Measures for Generative Artificial Intelligence Services apply when generative AI is used to provide generated text, images, audio or video to the public in mainland China; internal R&D/application that is not offered to the mainland public is excluded (Art. 2). “Provider” expressly includes an organization/person providing a generative-AI service through an API (Art. 22). A local product wrapping an overseas model is therefore likely within scope when offered to the public; the model's overseas location does not itself shift the provider obligations away from the product operator.
- Relevant obligations include lawful/content-safe output and protection of privacy, personal information, intellectual property and business secrets (Art. 4); provider responsibility for information content and personal-information processing, plus a user service agreement (Art. 9); protection of prompts and usage records, no unnecessary collection or unlawful retention/disclosure, and handling access/correction/deletion requests (Art. 11); generated-content labeling where the deep-synthesis rules apply (Art. 12); illegal-content handling and reporting (Art. 14); complaints and reports (Art. 15); and stable, continuous service (Art. 13).
- A service with “public opinion attributes or social mobilization capacity” must conduct the applicable security assessment and complete algorithm filing/change/cancellation procedures (Art. 17). The exact classification and launch procedure should be confirmed before public release rather than inferred from the overseas base model's status.
- Regulators may request explanations and necessary technical/data support about training-data sources, scale/types, labeling rules and algorithm mechanism (Art. 19). A black-box overseas provider may make this operationally difficult; the vendor contract and architecture should preserve enough auditability and cooperation to respond.
- For generative-AI services supplied from outside China that do not comply with Chinese law or the Interim Measures, CAC may notify relevant institutions to take technical or other necessary measures (Art. 20). Thus a gateway can change the network path, but it does not legalize a noncompliant service or guarantee durable access.

## 3. Technical feasibility vs. compliant operation

- **Technically feasible:** the app calls a vendor backend; that backend calls the overseas LLM and streams the response back. Users need no separately installed VPN, and API keys remain server-side.
- **Not equivalent to assured availability:** if the user connects directly to an overseas gateway, traffic still traverses cross-border networks; if a mainland relay is added, that relay performs the outbound transfer. Either design can face latency, packet loss, DNS/TLS reachability, regulatory intervention, vendor outages, and account/policy restrictions.
- **Not a compliance shortcut:** the operator centralizes responsibility for user notice/consent, minimization, incident response, overseas-recipient due diligence, cross-border mechanism/threshold tracking, content controls, complaints, and possible security assessment/algorithm filing. The UI must accurately say where data goes and who receives it; it should not imply mainland storage if prompts or logs leave China.
- **Practical release boundary:** an internal prototype using synthetic or non-personal test data is materially different from a public commercial release. Before production, obtain China-specific legal review; inventory every field/file/log sent abroad; determine whether sensitive personal information, minors' data, important data or regulated-industry data may appear; select/complete the transfer mechanism; implement separate cross-border consent and rights workflows; contract for no training/limited retention/deletion/subprocessors/security incidents; and assess generative-AI security/algorithm filing and content-labeling obligations.

## Limits

This review is limited to the named privacy/data-export and generative-AI rules and five official-domain web searches. It does not resolve sector-specific rules (finance, healthcare, education, state secrets, mapping, automotive data, minors, etc.), telecom/ICP licensing, foreign-investment restrictions, export-control/sanctions issues, the overseas model vendor's own territory/account policies, or the legality of any particular network implementation. Those require fact-specific review.
